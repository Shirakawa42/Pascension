# Strategy representation and training coverage audit, 2026-09-26

The current training can reach the normal legal actions, but that does **not** establish that it explores or understands every useful strategy. A new isolated fixture confirms that V7 still forgets useful, legitimately revealed personal-deck information. There are also clear reasons to measure activation timing, conditional effect success, and multi-card sequences before interpreting a low acquisition win rate as evidence that a card is bad.

Scope: source review of the frozen V7 policy and HostV6 observation/action contract, plus one tiny CPU fixture referencing the existing frozen DLL. No GPU work, game batch, checkpoint update, live process control, host rebuild, or pinned-source edit. This complements the parent's fresh behavior/statistics probes; it does not claim to have measured current frequencies for all the scenarios below.

## Newly reproduced: personal top-card knowledge is lost

Two constructed positions have the same personal deck multiset, but opposite Crystal/Blaster deck order. In both, the actor legally plays **Duplication Fabricator**, sees both players' top cards, then copies the same opposing Crystal. Immediately afterward, all **4,160 floats**—observation, candidates and legal mask—are exactly equal. However, the player legitimately knows that its own next draw is Blaster in one position and Crystal in the other. Playing Cinder Scars next draws those different cards.

| Verified property | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Revealed personal top card | Blaster | Crystal |
| Fabricator effect copied | Opponent Crystal | Opponent Crystal |
| Current complete policy input | Exactly equal | Exactly equal |
| Next Cinder Scars draw | Blaster | Crystal |

[Raw result](strategy-own-top-alias.json), [reproduction source](strategy-alias-probe/Program.cs), [separate project](strategy-alias-probe/Probe.csproj). The frozen host SHA-256 is `028cfd00a1f7737f2d70847a501f69e3e094ce9cfa56a99bc94861b1f1714cfd`. These are constructed fixtures followed by real accepted actions, **not a full initial-seed-to-position replay or a measured win-rate effect**. The fixture exits nonzero if the exact alias disappears, so it can be reused to check a future representation repair.

[Compact policy-input export](strategy-own-top-inputs.json) contains both cases with flat layout 2,048 observation + 2,048 candidate + 64 mask floats for independent CPU checkpoint scoring.

The underlying reason is explicit: `CenterKnowledge` only captures `soi.scry` and `soi.reorder` center-deck decisions; broad reveal events clear its knowledge. It has no personal-deck ledger. The observation counts the owner's deck as a multiset. V7 is feedforward and receives no recurrent state. The actual Fabricator effect reveals personal top cards without removing them. [Knowledge ledger](../experiments/HostV6/CenterKnowledge.cs#L24), [count channels](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L44), [Fabricator](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsDuelSet.cs#L652), [deck-top convention](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEngine.cs#L2009), [V7 trunk](../experiments/choice_policy_v7.py#L31).

This is a representation limitation, not an information leak or broken optimizer. It matters when deciding whether a draw will produce currency or power, sequencing a draw before another effect, and remembering publicly placed deck-top cards from Maglev Tunnels. A future repair should record authorized events/decisions per observer and invalidate on shuffle; it must not read hidden deck order. Dash's ordinary return is immediately followed by its own draw, so the existence of Dash alone does not establish a persistent planning failure. [Maglev placement](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEngine.cs#L579), [Dash sequence](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsDuelSet.cs#L410).

## Newly reproduced: Reactor's original/copy source can be aliased

An additional structural fixture places the same Reactor Drone and Ojas in identical public zones and queues the actual registered Reactor effect under either the Reactor source or the copier source. At the mode decision, **all 4,160 input floats are identical**. Selecting mode 2 gives three gems in both cases, but `BanishAtCleanup` becomes true only with the physical Reactor source. This directly verifies that the missing source field can matter to the choice's consequence. It is a constructed parked-effect fixture using reflection, **not a full legally replayed pair of histories**, and must not be described as one. [Result](strategy-reactor-source-alias.json), [fixture `--reactor` mode](strategy-alias-probe/Program.cs).

[Compact Reactor policy-input export](strategy-reactor-source-inputs.json) uses the same layout. Original option ordinal 0 means two gems; ordinal 1 means three gems. Warpquartz also retains the original `ctx` when resolving each banished card's play effect, so its copied Reactor does not become a physical Reactor source merely because it banished that card. [Warpquartz execution](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsDuelSet.cs#L637).

The rule is deliberate: Reactor's three-gem option schedules its own cleanup banish only if `ctx.Source.DefId == reactor_drone_duel` and that source is in the player's play zone. Ojas/other copy flows retain the copier's context when resolving the copied effect. Consequently, a copied Reactor's three-gem choice has no self-banish drawback; ordinary Reactor's same choice trades away the card. [Reactor rule](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsDuelSet.cs#L519), [copy context](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEffects.cs#L834).

The encoder passes neither the current resolving source nor a public initiating-action history. The options have no card `DefId`, cost or effect semantics; their vectors differ at ordinal column 25. A recent initiating play may often be inferable from other state differences in normal games, so this does **not** mean all copied Reactor decisions are necessarily misplayed. It does mean source-sensitive strategy is not exactly represented. Encoding a public/own resolving-source identity or a typed `physical_reactor_self_banish` flag would be a narrow versioned repair; reading an arbitrary hidden effect stack would not be acceptable. [Candidate encoding](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L253), [cleared staged history](../experiments/HostV6/Adapter.cs#L130).

Shard Defiant differs: its Keep and Banish modes both carry the **revealed card identity**, so the candidate card embedding knows which card is at stake; their ordinal separates the alternatives. The optional repeat is represented by a real selection versus completion action. Comet's Keep option is correctly disabled. These are still small ordinal/binary choices without typed benefits, but the newly found Reactor source alias should not be generalized to them. [Defiant modes and repeat](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsHorizonSet.cs#L657).

## What the current curriculum does and does not cover

- **Heroes:** 75% of training games externally assign one of all 20 ordered distinct-hero pairs; 25% retain policy draft. This repairs hero starvation, but is not a curriculum over relics, deck archetypes or late-game positions. Same-hero mirrors are excluded by the game's draft and are not missing legal matchups. [Routing](../experiments/HostV5/HeroSetupRouting.cs#L10), [pair sampling](../experiments/HostV5/HeroCurriculum.cs#L53).
- **Relics/destinies:** V7 adds 6% relic and 2% destiny uniform-mixture probability mass **at ordinary legal choice menus**. The mass is not a percentage of games. It does not force early drafting of a coherent deck, successful activation, or continued use. Bonus relic/destiny decisions are kind 12 and receive no special mixture. [Exact mixture](../experiments/choice_policy_v7.py#L69), [action kinds](../experiments/HostV6/Adapter.cs#L288).
- **Volos:** four independent state-conditioned residual scores fix the former tiny-ordinal representation bottleneck. The early V7 frozen probe found meaningful probability separation and selections of all four modes. That demonstrates improved distinguishability and some use, not optimal allocation of gems in all contexts. [Head](../experiments/choice_policy_v7.py#L27), [early V7 probe](v7-pilot-choice-coverage.json).
- **Opponents:** ordinary self-play is mixed with archived/champion opponents. With the configured 25% archive lanes, this diversifies historical policies but is not an adversarial best-response population or proof of an equilibrium. Current training is still learning from its own family's strategy distribution. [Opponent selection](../train_campaign.py#L217), [actual pilot configuration](v7-pilot-choice-coverage.json).
- **Credit:** complete-episode terminal returns allow long-term strategy credit without discounting it away. They do not tell the learner directly which delayed conditional effect or investment was responsible for winning. Censored games are excluded rather than assigned invented outcomes. That is sound accounting, but it does not solve sparse discovery. [Returns and rollout contract](../learning_rollout.py#L43).

## Gameplay paths that need separate evidence

“Available” below means represented as legal engine actions, not demonstrated competence. The earlier coverage probes established appearances/selections of every active relic and all active destiny definitions across populations; they did not validate every strategy combination. [Coverage investigation](choice-coverage-investigation-2026-09-26.md), [source audit and targeted engine tests](choice-coverage-source-audit-2026-09-26.md).

| Gameplay path | Available information/actions | Specific competence test before declaring weak/bad |
|---|---|---|
| Fast attack / power race | Health, power, attack choices, owned cards and market are visible. | Attack lethal face instead of unnecessary champion destruction; distinguish immediate fast-play damage from permanent deck investment. |
| Mastery race / Infinity Shard | Mastery, Infinity identity, Focus and threshold cards are present. | Reach M30 while retaining a playable Infinity Shard, sequence mastery before Infinity, and avoid banishing the win condition without compensation. Count actual M30 resolutions, not acquisitions. |
| Defensive mastery/control | Shield candidates, own dynamic shield, doubling flags, champions and public damage appear. | Save the correct amount against face and Testudo-related champion damage; use Datic Robes discard defense and Praetorian-02 at relevant times. Full-health healing can still produce power. |
| Champion board engine | Champion identities, exhaustion, damage, defense and count are present. | Play/reset/exhaust/copy in useful order; make threshold-dependent General Decurion/Unknown God combinations work. A champion merely entering play is insufficient. |
| Thin-deck Ko | Preview targets, HP cost, own collection and banish count are present. | Remove weak cards after obtaining a viable currency/damage engine; avoid destroying all economy. A paid banish is neither automatically correct nor automatically a bug. |
| Rez shop manipulation | Four known center-top cards after Scry/reorder, market identities and reroll cost are present. | Actually use the revealed order for reroll/warp decisions, and distinguish access to a valuable card from indiscriminate expensive rerolling. |
| Permanent buying versus mercenary fast-play | Separate action kinds and temporary candidate flags exist. | Account for remaining rounds, immediate lethal, allegiance/unify contribution, and possible later retention. Zero permanent purchases may coexist with useful fast-play. |
| Talons healing-to-power | Conversion/doubling/overflow flags are explicitly observed. | Heal at cap when it grants power; sequence Talons/Lifebloom/Nectar before healing. Do not label cap healing globally wasted. |
| Faction Allegiance | Own exact faction ownership counts include intended temporary fast-plays. | Recruit/play the fourth faction card before an Allegiance payoff; preserve the distinction between collection count and played-this-turn count. |
| Unify / Dominion | Faction and ally-play counts, hand and played cards are present. | Reveal/play the required cards, preserve multi-faction Prisms correctly, and get the actual conditional bonus rather than simply owning the synergy cards. |
| Mixed-faction destinies | Faction counters and owned destiny are present. | Delay Forged in Flame, Paradigm Shift, Crystal Gate or Biotech until their conditions are met; verify a benefit-producing exhaust. |
| Cost/type-history destinies | Current zones are encoded, but exact `PlayedThisTurn` identities are not a dedicated history channel. | Track successful Last City, Absorption Grid, Advanced Weapons/Medicine, and Unconditional Conscription activations after cards move or are banished. |
| Warpquartz banish/copy | Banish target identities, hand/discard zones and running banish count are present. | Banish earlier, then use the relic; distinguish useful copy targets from sacrificing valuable draw/economy; evaluate its M20 multi-target sequence. |
| Doom Gate / monster rewards | Monster identities and legal attacks are present; pending-attacker count is only aggregate. | Flood, play/exhaust the gate, select the right reward/target and exploit immunity; count new reward branches and extra relic/destiny use. |
| Extra turn / reset loops | Once-per-game extra-turn flag, exhaustion and mastery are present. | Cross M20 before Slipstream resolves; exploit resets without wasting the reset on the wrong champion or empty conditional effect. |
| Copy/repeat choices | All currently legal targets remain candidates. | Prefer targets based on immediate effects and their ordering; recognize source-specific copy behavior and avoid treating every copied option as equivalent to a physical play. |
| Public-top draw planning | Personal revealed-top memory is missing, verified above. | A repaired representation should distinguish the exact fixture and then show useful planning in a frozen matched test. |
| Opponent deck inference | Opponent current discard/play/champions are visible; long-term public acquisition/banish history is absent. | Remember that an opponent lost Infinity Shard or acquired large damage even after those cards leave visible zones. Current low-memory scores cannot settle such strategies. |
| Very large tutor/warp/banish menus | Paging preserves legal reachability. | Choose valuable later-page targets; record how often they are offered, reached and selected. Existing small-game probes seeing zero page actions do not validate later-page competence. |

## Particularly suspicious mechanisms to measure

**Premature conditional exhaust is a high-priority behavioral hypothesis.** The engine offers any unexhausted, affordable destiny/champion with an exhaust effect without checking whether the effect's condition currently succeeds. Thus acquiring a useful destiny and immediately exhausting it for nothing can make it appear weak in acquisition-outcome statistics. V7's extra acquisition exploration does not directly teach waiting. Track `(condition false now, could become true later, exhausted anyway)`, then actual effects gained. Do not remove these legal actions merely because a heuristic thinks waiting is preferable. [Legal exhaust menus](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEngine.cs#L1825), [destiny conditions](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsHorizonSet.cs#L440).

A narrow whitelist of a **free, single false conditional effect** can identify a currently fruitless activation while preserving it for later. Do not apply this to a compound card merely because one condition is false: another effect may give immediate unconditional value. Exhausting changes status even if no reward resolves, and Unknown God can duplicate the effect. Deferring a provably fruitless exhaust is a potential strategic simplification, but filtering it still changes the behavior distribution/action abstraction and must be evaluated and versioned; it is not a bit-for-bit transport optimization. [Exhaust execution](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEngine.cs#L715).

**Play-order effects are not covered by card appearances.** The fixed initial prior gives hand plays +1 and Focus +0.5, while exhaust/buy/hero actions have zero. Learned logits can override it. Still, tally whether the policy plays Talons after heals, Infinity before crossing a reachable mastery threshold, or Warpquartz before other banishes. These are testable hypotheses, not proof that the priors currently cause mistakes. [Prior](../learning_model.py#L24), [prior application](../experiments/choice_policy_v7.py#L64).

**Representational compression remains outside the memory fixture.** Only 24 public entities receive individual status records, while aggregate counts and all legal candidates remain available. Only the last 16 selections have ordered trace records; all selected card counts are also retained. Pending monster attacks are counted without identifying each newly revealed attacker. Context and title are hashes, not parsed effect/source semantics. Count occurrences before paying for a larger representation. [Entity/trace capacities](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L23), [overflow](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L199), [hashes](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L147).

**The value trunk has no candidate-set input.** Newly revealed candidate-only identities do not affect the value estimate. Except for Volos's explicit head and category-mixture normalization, candidate logits are scored independently against a common state query. This makes some menu-dependent continuation reasoning harder; it is an architectural limitation, not evidence that ordinary choices are impossible. [Policy scoring](../experiments/choice_policy_v7.py#L31).

**Do not overstate missing history.** A generic “played a champion” predicate can often be inferred from total faction plays minus faction ally plays, because champions are the non-allies. Ordinary played mercenaries are also present in the play zone. The most defensible gaps concern exact identity/status after movement, personal top-card knowledge, copy context and large-board compression. A claim that every such destiny is unobservable would be too strong. [Faction counters](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsState.cs#L52), [encoded counters](../experiments/HostV6/Encoder.cs#L140).

## Interpretation and next checks

A rare action is legitimately undesirable only relative to its situation and continuation policy. Zero Comet fast-plays and inactive replaced relics are expected rules; zero useful conditional activations after many eligible turns are suspicious. Neither high nor low acquisition win rate establishes causality: mastery timing, winning positions, remaining game length, opponent and hero all confound it. [Comet restriction](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsDuelSet.cs#L162), [active-set audit](choice-coverage-source-audit-2026-09-26.md).

The least disruptive next checks are frozen sidecar event traces for **offered → selected → played/exhausted → condition succeeded → actual payoff**, stratified by hero, mastery and game phase. For suspected bad choices, use paired continuations from the same legal position and multiple fresh randomness streams; an intervention score still measures how well the current policy continues from the alternative. Stronger claims about an inherently bad card require optimizing the alternative strategy too.

A personal/public knowledge ledger or typed conditional-readiness features may be worth a later versioned continuation, but this audit alone does not justify interrupting the running learner or silently changing its observation identity. Preserve old checkpoints and the shared charged-time budget; test exact migration behavior and frozen strength before adoption. No current result warrants saying every gameplay path is covered or that all remaining low-use cards are intrinsically bad.
