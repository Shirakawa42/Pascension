# Search depth versus learning efficiency: primary-source review

2026-10-03. Read-only literature investigation for the current zero-depth, width512 policy. No model load, training update, GPU work, Windows process, runtime edit or campaign change occurred. The reported mean of approximately12.4 rounds after1.3million games is user context, not an independently measured strength result here. No paper below validates a ten-round Shards benchmark.

A direct policy can learn long-term consequences from complete-game rewards without explicitly simulating future moves when it acts. Conversely, adding search at deployment changes decisions but does not itself update the network. The relevant question is whether additional computation produces stronger play and better policy/value targets per elapsed hour.

## What the original research establishes

| Primary source | Established result | Relevance and limit here |
|---|---|---|
| [AlphaZero, Silver et al.,2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.01815v1) | MCTS root visit probabilities train the policy; actual game outcomes train the value. Its reported training used800 simulations per move and thousands of TPUs. | Evidence for search as a policy-improvement teacher, rather than simply adding a depth parameter to PPO. Its hardware, deterministic perfect-information games and game counts cannot predict this machine's optimum. |
| [Expert Iteration, Anthony et al.,2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/d8e1344e27a5b08cdfd5d027d9b8d6de-Paper.pdf) | A search expert labels states visited by the fast apprentice; the apprentice learns those decisions and then helps improve search. The paper reports stronger learning than REINFORCE on Hex and notes that generating search labels dominates runtime. Online dataset aggregation reduces repeated labeling work. | Best architectural match for keeping fast policy self-play while selectively spending compute on better decisions. A teacher must first outperform its apprentice; greater depth alone is insufficient. Hex findings do not establish Shards strength. |
| [Reanalyse/MuZero Unplugged, Schrittwieser et al.,2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/e8258e5140317ff36c7f8225a3bf9590-Paper.pdf) | Re-planning stored states generates updated policy and value targets. This differs from replaying the same targets for more gradient steps. Atari experiments vary fresh interaction versus reanalysis under substantially different data budgets. | Supports allocating some compute to improving existing targets rather than only collecting more games. Data efficiency is not automatically wall-time efficiency. Its learner/model objectives already support these targets; it is not a drop-in replay mode for this PPO learner. |
| [MuZero, Schrittwieser et al.,2020](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.08265v2) | A learned dynamics representation predicts rewards, policies and values used in search without a supplied environment model. | A later option for neural batched planning, but this project already has an exact rules engine. Learning another simulator adds model error and engineering/training cost; the paper does not establish that this replacement is necessary here. |
| [Gumbel planning, Danihelka et al.,2022](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bERaNdoegnO) | AlphaZero can fail to improve its policy when root actions are unvisited. Sampling actions without replacement and policy-improvement targets improve low-simulation results on the tested domains. | Particularly relevant if a small search budget is tried. Inspect root-action coverage and improvement-target construction. Guarantees involving estimated action values do not certify their accuracy or transfer a perfect-information algorithm to hidden cards. |
| [ReBeL, Brown et al.,2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/c61f571dbd2fb949d3fe5ae1608dd48b-Paper.pdf) | Search and learning operate on public belief states, including distributions over private states, with convergence results under the algorithm's two-player zero-sum assumptions. | Correctness reference for hidden hands/order. Public deck composition is not knowledge of the actual hidden arrangement. A shallow sampled-world average is not ReBeL and inherits none of its equilibrium guarantees. |
| [Student of Games, Schmid et al.,2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651118/) | Combines belief-based CFR search, self-play and policy/value targets, with results in both perfect- and imperfect-information games. More search can improve a fixed trained network, while training/search consistency matters. | Confirms that inference computation and network learning are separate axes. It is a substantially different solver/learner, not a small PPO configuration change. |
| [Information Set MCTS, Cowling et al.,2012](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf) | Independent determinizations can incorrectly allow different decisions in observationally indistinguishable states: strategy fusion. Sharing information-set decisions helps in some domains; card-game branching can negate the benefit. | Search must use only the acting player's information, share choices until observations distinguish hidden worlds, and model the opponent's information too. No universal speed or strength guarantee. |
| [DeepNash, Perolat et al.,2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378) | Reaches expert Stratego play using model-free game-theoretic reinforcement learning without search. | Counterexample to “no lookahead means strong strategy cannot be learned.” Its R-NaD objective is different from PPO; it does not prove the current implementation will converge. |
| [Policy-space response oracles, Lanctot et al.,2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/3323fe11e9595c09af38fe67567a9394-Paper.pdf) | Policies can overfit their training partners. Approximate best responses against policy mixtures and empirical payoff analysis address generalization across opponents. | Opponent diversity and archive selection may offer improvement without multiplying every environment transition. Existing archived opponents do not automatically implement PSRO or prevent forgetting. |
| [PPO, Schulman et al.,2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347) | Alternates current-policy data collection and multiple minibatch optimization epochs; combines clipped policy ratios with value loss and optional entropy. Reported settings vary by benchmark. | Learning rate, entropy, reuse and value fitting deserve measured ablations. Lower training loss, low mean KL, more accepted updates or higher GPU utilization alone do not certify playing strength. |

## Recommended next decision: an inference from these sources

First verify progress against frozen opponents, with balanced hero matchups and both seats. A stable self-play game length can coexist with improving offense and defense; shortening games can also mean worse defense. Mean rounds alone establishes neither a plateau nor a desirable objective. Outcome score, confidence bounds and a review of complete games should drive the decision. [Agarwal et al.,2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Paper.pdf) documents how RL point estimates can support misleading conclusions without uncertainty analysis.

Then use a bounded search pilot on frozen real states. Compare genuinely different continuations, preserve legal alternatives, and measure hidden-information invariance and whole-game strength against the frozen policy. Use elapsed compute budgets or node budgets, not only raw wrapper-action depth: UI/selection continuations and strategically consequential plays are different units. Exact immediate tactical lines and deterministic same-turn segments are sensible starting cases; unknown draws introduce chance/information boundaries. These are project proposals, not tested improvements.

If the teacher is stronger, collect its policy distributions and suitable value targets on a selected, diverse fraction of real states, retain their provenance, and distill them into the existing fast policy in an explicitly versioned experiment. Keep ordinary policy-generated PPO rows and teacher targets separate. The present learner verifies stored likelihoods and values by recomputing the original network. Selecting actions with a search distribution q while logging the original network probability pi_old(a) and value can pass that numerical check, even though the samples were generated by q rather than pi_old and the PPO estimator has incorrect behavior provenance. Logging the true q(a) instead would generally fail the existing network-recompute check. Search-generated PPO data therefore require a deliberately redesigned estimator and provenance validation; the current verifier does not guarantee detection of falsely attributed search actions. Compare the distilled zero-depth policy after equal total wall time, including label generation, optimizer work and evaluation. Also measure the undistilled policy plus search at an equal deployment decision budget.

If the teacher is not stronger, deeper search may merely amplify an inaccurate leaf critic or repeat a poor continuation. Investigate critic calibration, exploration, legal-action coverage and opponent diversity before paying for longer searches. A modest, separately tested learning-rate/entropy/reuse change is more focused than increasing model size or changing many settings together. No source establishes the best numerical values for this game.

The evidence favors selective, validated search supervision as a promising next experiment while preserving fast self-play. It does not justify switching the live campaign to deep search, guarantee a faster win, or establish a universal rounds target.
