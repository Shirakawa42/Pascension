## Automation that preserves the learning problem

The reliable default is **exactly one legal reachable continuation**, detected before encoding/upload. Execute that action through `Submit` and continue pumping until a real choice, terminal result, or administrative cap. Keep separate policy-decision, wrapper-step, engine-submission and automated-step counters. Existing split interval settling already automates an integer once its interval is a singleton. [Adapter.Step/SettleSplit/Rebuild](../Host/Adapter.cs).

Examples include a required single-option decision, FINISH when no unselected option remains and completion is legal, and a singleton integer allocation. An optional single-option decision still offers select versus FINISH; it is not forced. An option marked Required is not proof that all other options, order, or amounts are forced. A candidate list that includes concede contains a real additional action and must not be falsely counted as a singleton. Broad forced subset/permutation collapsing requires proof against decision-order semantics; begin with singleton candidates. [Exact staged action contract](../Host/CONTRACT.md).

Do not automatically play all starter/resource cards, focus, exhaust champions, fast-play mercenaries, purchase the cheapest card, or end a turn. Their ordering, mastery thresholds, resources, temporary ownership and future interaction with draw/banish/copy effects are choices. Focus explicitly spends a gem and changes mastery. Play moves a particular instance and resolves its effects; arbitrary same-definition cards can differ in public status and relationships. These heuristics can be studied as named policy ablations later, but have no correctness proof or general strength guarantee. [LegalActions/Focus/Play implementation](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Engine/ShardsEngine.cs), [card/effect registry](../../../Assets/Scripts/Shards/Content/ShardsBaseSet.cs).

Whether forced automation is faster is empirical: it saves policy/transfer/record rows, but adds candidate scans and may create variable CPU work per lane. Benchmark on/off with an identical deterministic non-forced action schedule, matching final state hashes, outcomes, submission traces and public-memory observations at retained decision boundaries. Count forced decisions and their share, then measure full-pipeline wall time. A zero observed forced share means scanning can only add overhead for that cohort. A hard loop bound and cap checks must apply while pumping forced actions as well.

## Representation and canonicalization

Alphabetical catalog IDs are already used by the historical encoder; its hand channels are unordered exact counts, so sorting hand cards gives no extra benefit there. Reuse a fixed ordered catalog, exact integer counts, and canonicalized **encoded copies** for genuinely unordered groups. Do not sort live engine hand/deck/target/decision lists; list order can drive draw, reorder and submitted selection behavior. [Encoder layout](../Host/CONTRACT.md), [determinism constraints](../../../CLAUDE.md).

Retain every publicly distinguishable instance: definition, owner, zone, damage, ready/temporary flags, revealed position, attack/source/target links and selected order where applicable. Keep a back-map from encoded entity/candidate positions to exact engine actions. Same-definition champions with different damage or queued targets are not interchangeable. Deduplicating two actions only because their visible feature vectors collide hides missing information and can delete meaningful choices. Begin without action deduplication; add it only with explicit equivalence proofs and paired transition tests.

A set encoder can remove arbitrary unordered-list dependence while preserving all per-entity input records; learned pooling is a model transformation, not permission to truncate records. Deep Sets supplies the mathematical motivation for permutation-invariant set functions, but does not establish that any particular Shards encoding is sufficient or stronger. [Deep Sets paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114).

The old 2,048-vector/64-candidate schema intentionally omits public history, banished identities, entities beyond 24 records, remembered revealed tops, full staged order and deferred hit allocations. It cannot be relabeled complete. The new information audit must cover the pending-input owner's visible/private-authorized data and persistent public knowledge, with collision/overflow failure rather than silent clipping. Unknown opponent hands, unknown deck order and RNG remain unavailable; previously revealed and still logically known identities/positions remain available until the rules invalidate that knowledge. [Old omissions](../training-contract.md), [visibility invariant](../../../CLAUDE.md).

## GPU and host execution recommendations

1. Keep actor/learner computation, sampled log probabilities and rollout tensors on the GPU. Batch publication and transfers into contiguous buffers. Preallocate owned pinned input/output staging; reuse it after explicit copy completion. NVIDIA advises minimizing host/device transfers and keeping intermediate data on-device; actual transfer cost remains part of the workload. [CUDA best practices](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-c-best-practices-guide/index.html#data-transfer-between-host-and-device).
2. Start with eager IEEE FP32 and measured fixed batches 128/256/512/1024; test captured actors on the same shapes and frozen weights. CUDA graphs reduce dispatch overhead but require stable addresses/shapes/control flow and long-lived input/output storage. Bucket live lanes when beneficial, preserve original mappings, and never store padding rows as experience. [PyTorch CUDA graph constraints](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/cuda.html#cuda-graphs), [existing adaptive actor](../adaptive_actor.py).
3. Pinned inputs support asynchronous upload, but manually pinning fresh tensors each step can erase the benefit. Overlap additionally needs independent streams/device capability and correct ownership. Never overwrite pinned H2D source memory before completion, or consume nonblocking D2H output before synchronization. [PyTorch transfer tutorial](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/intermediate/pinmem_nonblock.html).
4. Sweep actual CPU workers alongside batch size. Eight physical cores do not imply eight is always best; historic encoded CPU-only results favored 8–12, but integrated actor results include Python and transfer costs. Keep Torch CPU threads small to avoid competing with simulation. Use span shared-memory publication where byte-parity holds; its historic 23.3× cached copy gain is not a whole-training gain. [Host measurements](../Host/PERFORMANCE.md).
5. Keep actor weights frozen for each collection, retain complete-episode terminal credit, and preserve owned rollout rows until learning finishes. Current synchronous generations make probability verification straightforward. An asynchronous actor/learner redesign introduces stale-policy/off-policy issues and deserves a separate contract; removing search does not require it. [Learning contract](../training-contract.md), [rollout ownership](../learning_rollout.py).
6. Treat BF16/TF32/mixed precision as optional numerical experiments after a correct complete-input FP32 baseline. The previous campaign stopped once actor/learner selected-log-probability mismatch exceeded its guard; existing hardware/API research documents why Blackwell alone does not guarantee a desired FP32 Tensor Core path. Do not relax PPO numerical checks for throughput. [FP32 research](fp32-throughput-research-2026-09-26.md), [previous contract](../training-contract.md).

The proposed benchmark should report setup separately; timed completed/capped/held lanes, policy and engine decisions/s, games/s, host step/encoding/IPC time, actor upload/inference/download time, response bytes, peak allocated/reserved VRAM and sampled driver utilization. Capture graph/eager output parity separately from stochastic trajectory parity. Use alternating repeated configurations and frozen seed/weight identities when selecting defaults; a single short warm run is a smoke result, not a definitive ranking. Include learner time in a later real-training pilot once authorized launch/configuration is concrete.

## Strength gate after training

Freeze the currently installed AI's weights, rules, search settings and runtime hash before beginning. Evaluate the trained direct-action challenger against that **current installed AI**, with its actual search/automation behavior, not just against the new model's random initial policy. Use held-out seed pairs, swap seats, balance hero matchups, record all terminals/caps, and publish a confidence interval and unresolved-outcome bounds. Also compare against archived direct-action policies to detect forgetting. Match search-free challenger inference to training; allowing it new search at evaluation would answer a different question. Existing final-evaluation code supplies outcome/seat/sampling patterns, but the new observation identity needs its own adapter. [Frozen evaluation contract](../training-contract.md), [installed-policy verifier](../verify_packaged_ai.py), [production search evaluation](../final_evaluation.py).

A faster training loop and complete visible inputs are readiness improvements, not evidence that the resulting policy is stronger. Promotion should require a predeclared lower confidence bound above 50% against the frozen current AI, with no unresolved cases silently converted to draws.

## Implemented preparation and measured findings

The separate [benchmark harness](../../ZeroDepthTraining/benchmark.py) now runs frozen real-engine actors, paired deterministic automation checks, completed-cohort drains, GPU stage timing, owned-memory/likelihood checks and optional **discarded fabricated-target** optimizer probes. It records source/binary/policy fingerprints, censored and unresolved games, active versus padded rows, and exact action replay files on host failures. It does not launch outcome learning.

The new input is 24,576 observation floats plus 64×48 candidate floats and 64 mask floats: **110,848 upload bytes per lane**, about 6.66× the historical compact upload. A frozen initial policy with 24 histogram channels and 512 static effect features was used; this is a different model and workload from the old 116-games/s exercise benchmark. [Input catalog](../../ZeroDepthTraining/Host/Encoder.cs), [model](../../ZeroDepthTraining/model.py).

A first functional batch-128 GPU check passed probability/value parity and a discarded 127-row PPO update. Selected behavior log-probability error was `1.19e-7`; learned disposable parameters and Adam state were finite, and the original frozen model's checksum stayed unchanged. This validates the executed path, not learning strength. [Functional smoke artifact](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/functional-gpu-smoke.json).

Profiling exposed expensive repeated setup reconstruction and string sorting; cached immutable catalog/setup data and numeric sort keys removed those costs. A sampled EventPipe trace of the next version attributed **47.9% of benchmark-callback sampled thread time** to zeroing buffers, motivating checked reused-buffer prefix clearing. Encoder inclusive sampled time was about 77.2% within those callbacks. Sampling includes native execution and is not a precise hardware CPU timer. [Profile summary](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/cpu-profile-before-prefix-clear.json), [official dotnet-trace documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/diagnostics/dotnet-trace).

The intermediate frozen 12-configuration sweep measured approximately 19,095 active decisions/s for captured 128×8, 23,846 for 256×8, and 24,121 for 512×8. Short larger-batch runs had many unfinished games, so their first-completion games/s are explicitly unsuitable as steady completed-game rates. Eager versus captured stochastic trajectories differed; these are descriptive systems measurements, not a paired action/strength comparison. [Intermediate sweep](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/frozen-gpu-sweep-v1.json).

Forced automation applied **308 of 51,765 wrapper choices (0.595%)** in 128 paired exercise games. Final state/outcome/submission digests matched exactly. One timing pair was 0.6882s without and 0.6935s with automation, a 0.8% difference that does not establish a material slowdown or gain. Forced automation remains a safe representation choice; blanket starter/focus/purchase automation has no equivalence proof. [128-game parity artifact](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/automation-128-before-center-fix.json).

Longer complete-cohort probes found two real knowledge-tracker faults that shorter tests missed: a detached revealed card entered a public zone twice in the entity map, and a possibly-absent remembered center card was treated as a certain top card. The recorded 409×256 and 2,061×128 action traces reproduced both failures without GPU or learning, and guided targeted repairs. Final readiness/performance must use the fixed host and newly validated adaptive actor; earlier timing files remain historical preparation evidence.

## Final frozen-host validation

The definitive runs used the same frozen width-256 policy, IEEE FP32, CUDA graphs, 128 real-engine lanes and eight CPU workers. Every configuration requested 20 seconds, then drained its current cohort; setup and the initial reset are separate, while subsequent resets are included. No optimizer ran during these comparisons. All **2,432 GPU games completed naturally**, with zero censored or unfinished games at each configuration's stop. [Fixed actor artifact](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-frozen-fixed128.json), [adaptive actor artifact](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-frozen-adaptive128.json).

These are descriptive workload measurements: packing and skipping forced choices consume RNG differently and change sampled trajectories. Packed actors used about 38–43 microseconds of actor wall service per active decision, versus 59–60 for fixed actors. Fixed actor service occupied about 69% of collection wall time; packed actors occupied 58–59%, with CPU host roundtrips occupying 38%. CPU host telemetry fuses rule execution and encoding, so it cannot separate those costs. The sampled GPU-utilization medians were 25–29%. This pipeline still has substantial CPU, transfer and synchronization work. The initial full-lane CUDA-event probes measured median H2D intervals of 0.607–0.760 ms, inference plus sampling of 0.285–0.323 ms, and D2H below 0.004 ms. These isolated repeated-input GPU intervals exclude CPU staging and live host service. Selected likelihood and value differences against eager evaluation were zero in all four probes. Terminal lanes never enter retained rollout storage, and packed child-row mappings have separate owned-memory and GPU graph tests. [Measured probes](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-frozen-adaptive128.json), [adaptive tests](../../ZeroDepthTraining/tests/test_adaptive.py).

The final deterministic 128-game CPU automation pair again matched final state/outcome digests and all 46,251 engine submissions. Automation applied 308 of 51,765 wrapper choices (0.595%). One pair took 0.9307 s without and 0.8752 s with automation; together with opposite differences in earlier and GPU runs, this does not establish a consistent material throughput gain or penalty. Keep singleton automation optional and equivalent; there is no evidence supporting broader tactical automation. [Final paired CPU checks](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-frozen-fixed128.json).

The recommended prepared default is **128 lanes, eight CPU workers and captured active-lane buckets**. The maximum observed complete-cohort footprint was 63,190 policy rows, or 48.2% of the configured 131,072-row GPU store before archive exclusions. This is a measured initial-policy workload, not a guarantee for future games; capacity checks still stop instead of dropping or overwriting observations. A final fixed/adaptive comparison at 256 lanes was not performed; the historical 256/512 prefix sweeps do not justify increasing the prepared default. The approximately 29–33 completed games/s figures exclude learning, archive opponents and final evaluation, and must not be advertised as overall training throughput. [Prepared configuration](../../ZeroDepthTraining/train.py), [final collection artifacts](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-frozen-adaptive128.json).

A separate discarded optimizer probe exercised the final adaptive actor and GPU rollout on 128 real encoded rows with fabricated terminal utilities. It completed one optimizer step with zero behavior likelihood/value discrepancy, finite learned parameters and Adam state, and an unchanged original frozen model. The probe's unfinished games supply no real outcomes or strength evidence, and no learned checkpoint was retained. [Separate optimizer artifact](../../ZeroDepthTraining/results/final-adaptive-disposable-optimizer.json).
