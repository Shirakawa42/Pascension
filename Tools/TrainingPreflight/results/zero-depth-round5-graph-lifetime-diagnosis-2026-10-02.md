# Reused PPO graph failure: resolved benchmark graph-owner lifetime

The stopped-main normal-kernel throughput comparison was rejected. After two disposable warmup updates, the baseline accepted one PPO minibatch while the bundle accepted all153. Every raw observation/candidate/mask/packet, target, terminal result and sampled RNG state matched. This was not ordinary FP32 reduction variation.

The normal instrumentation reproduced the failure twice. Before update, complete policy/Adam state, Python/NumPy/Torch RNG, the permutation, and first-minibatch inputs were identical. First clipped gradients were bitwise equal for every non-embedding parameter; embedding differences were below7.6e-10. Immediately after Adam, trunk.0.weight differed by up to5,714,632 and its exp_avg by190.49. KL on minibatch2 became21,036,348. A later replay raised CUDA illegal memory access. Evidence is preserved in normal-reuse-progress.json and normal-reuse.log. That process exited; it never wrote campaign state.

The installed Torch2.14.0+cu130 is commit08187d9e0fba026dc8217405802ab5381dc88d90. Its [CUDAGraph::reset](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/08187d9e0fba026dc8217405802ab5381dc88d90/aten/src/ATen/cuda/CUDAGraph.cpp#L313) clears cuBLAS workspaces by capture stream before releasing graph pools. [cuBLAS workspace caching](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/08187d9e0fba026dc8217405802ab5381dc88d90/aten/src/ATen/cuda/CublasHandlePool.cpp) associates scratch allocations with handles/streams. The original actor and PPO graphs use Torch's shared default capture stream. Disposing an actor family while retaining PPO could therefore release the allocation containing cuBLAS scratch that the PPO replay still referenced. Subsequent optimizer restoration reused that storage. This source explanation is consistent with the observed moment corruption and eventual illegal access.

The empirical lifetime fix is confined to the off-repo benchmark: preconstruct both actor families before PPO capture and strongly retain both for the whole probe. Do not create/drop superseded shared-stream actor graphs after capturing PPO; release learner graphs first after all replay streams join. Two CPU weakref/ordering regressions verify this ownership contract.

Under normal native kernels, the retained-family replay completed both153-step warmups plus THREE same pair-0 native repeats and two bundle repeats. Every measured repeat retained all51,472 real decisions and completed153Adam steps/154,416example passes; all raw/target/terminal/sampled/post-update RNG evidence matched. First-two PPO metrics stayed identical, including healthy KL2=4.6490648e-5. The90RAW work bound stopped before the optional eighth cohort; no incomplete cohort was credited. Cleanup and subsequent withheld CPU reference loading extended process wall/RAW duration to101.5seconds. These instrumented timings are not throughput results.

A separate small reference then loaded the identical source policy/Adam and the lossless saved actual minibatches. Fresh eager and separate fresh graph policies matched the retained-run first-two KL/loss/entropy/norm exactly; every non-embedding gradient matched bitwise. Remaining native embedding gradient differences were≤8.15e-10, policy differences≤9.31e-10, Adam moment differences≤1.14e-13. No trunk corruption occurred. GPU work8.73RAWseconds, peak allocated992,860,160bytes/reserved1,323,302,912bytes. See fresh-two-step-reference.json and actual-pair0-minibatch-{0,1}.npz.

The live trainer keeps its actor/PPO graphs alive across generations and does not switch/dispose these benchmark families; the source checkpoint remains immutable and unchanged. These results support resuming that baseline and using the retained-owner benchmark for further performance decisions. They do not accept the optimization or demonstrate60trained games/s. A new authoritative four-pair probe must use the reviewed production-shaped append/verifier fallback guards, full131072capacity, unchanged real PPO and strict all-row/RNG/steps/pass checks.
